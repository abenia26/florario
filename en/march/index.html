March flowers: 8M, Saint Joseph and yellow flowers

Spring arrives in the north and autumn in the south. March brings the mimosa of 8M, Father’s Day in Spain and the Mexican version of yellow flowers on the 21st.

Dates for giving flowers in March

March has three dates in three weeks, and yellow is the color that connects them:

In Italy a sprig of mimosa has been given since 1946; in Russia and Eastern Europe tulips are typical. In Spain and Latin America 8M is above all a day of protest: if you give flowers, make them a recognition, not a substitute for listening.

It falls on Saint Joseph’s Day, the father of Jesus in Christian tradition. Flowers are not the most typical gift, but more and more people give sunflowers or a plant that lasts: a present that doesn’t wilt in a week.

The September 21 trend doubled: with spring arriving in the northern hemisphere, Mexico adopted a second yellow flowers day. Same song, same idea, different season.

  • March 8: in Italy people give mimosa; in Russia and Eastern Europe, tulips. In Spain and Latin America it is above all a day of protest. All the context is in the Women’s Day guide.
  • March 19: Father’s Day in Spain, Portugal, Italy, Bolivia and Honduras, for Saint Joseph. Ideas in the Father’s Day guide.
  • March 21: the spring yellow flowers in Mexico and Central America.

In Valencia, the Fallas also have their floral moment: the Offering to Our Lady of the Forsaken, on the 17th and 18th, when thousands of falleras bring bouquets that are arranged to form a giant mantle. It isn’t a gift between people, but it is one of the biggest gatherings of flowers of the year in Spain.

In Colombia, Ecuador and the Andes, the high-altitude climate lets growers produce roses, carnations, chrysanthemums, alstroemerias and hydrangeas all year round: that is why they are two of the world’s largest flower exporters. This table is a guide: the season changes with the area and the year, and greenhouses extend almost all of them.

In Spain spring gets going in earnest: tulips, daffodils, hyacinths, freesias and ranunculus at their best, and the last mimosas and violets. In Mexico the jacarandas start blooming in the streets, and flower shops are full of sunflowers, calla lilies and daisies, perfect for the 21st. In Argentina, Chile and Uruguay autumn begins, with dahlias, asters and the first chrysanthemums.

What to give in March

  • 8M: a sprig of mimosa or tulips, as a recognition, and always alongside real support, never instead of it.
  • Father’s Day: sunflowers, a bouquet in blue and white tones or a plant that lasts: a small olive tree, a succulent or an orchid.
  • March 21: sunflowers, tulips or yellow roses.
  • To celebrate spring: a bouquet of tulips and daffodils, each in its own vase so they don’t harm each other.

Tips for the month

March is the best month to buy tulips in Spain: they are in full season and cheap. If you mix them with daffodils, first leave the daffodils on their own in water for a few hours, because the sap they release shortens the life of other flowers.

For March 21, just like in September, sunflowers sell out in Mexico: order them a few days ahead. And if you want to remember all these dates next year, download the calendar with reminders.

Frequently asked questions

Which flowers are given in March?

Mimosa or tulips on March 8 (Women’s Day), sunflowers or plants on March 19 (Father’s Day in Spain) and yellow flowers on March 21 in Mexico.

Why are yellow flowers given on March 21?

Because it marks the arrival of spring in the northern hemisphere. Mexico adopted it as a second date for the yellow flowers trend, which in the south is celebrated on September 21.

Which flowers are in season in March in Spain?

Tulips, daffodils, ranunculus, freesias, hyacinths and violets, as well as flowering almond and cherry branches.

Are flowers given on Father’s Day?

More and more. Sunflowers, flowers in blue tones and houseplants are the favorite options.

What is the Fallas Flower Offering?

During the Fallas in Valencia, on March 17 and 18, thousands of falleras bring bouquets to Our Lady of the Forsaken, and they are used to build a huge floral mantle in the square.

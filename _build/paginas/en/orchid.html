What it means to give an orchid

The orchid is associated with elegance, admiration and delicate love. For centuries it was a rare flower: in Victorian England collectors sent expeditions to the Americas and Asia to find new species, and owning orchids was a sign of wealth. That sense of luxury and of “you are special” is still there.

By color, white conveys purity and respect, pink or fuchsia affection and admiration, and purple elegance. The bright blue orchids you see at florists are dyed: a blue phalaenopsis does not exist naturally.

A huge family

Orchids make up one of the largest plant families in the world, with more than 25,000 species. Their name comes from the Greek orchis, after the shape of the roots of some European species. Vanilla, for example, is the fruit of a Mexican orchid.

The one people give is almost always the phalaenopsis or moth orchid: it is easy to care for indoors, it is greenhouse-grown all year and its flowers stay open for weeks.

When to give an orchid

Because it is a greenhouse plant, you can give it in any month. It is an especially good choice for:

  • Mother’s Day and grandmothers: a gift that stays in the house and reminds them of you every time it blooms again.
  • Birthdays and housewarmings: no vase needed, and it lasts much longer than a bouquet.
  • Coworkers: it fits on a desk and has no scent, so it bothers no one.
  • Sympathy: a white orchid is a discreet gesture the family can keep.

How to care for an orchid

  1. Light without direct sun: near a window, but behind a curtain or a meter (3 ft) from the glass. If the leaves turn yellow or scorch, it is getting too much sun.
  2. Water by soaking: dip the pot in lukewarm water for about 10 minutes and let it drain completely. Standing water rots the roots.
  3. Check the roots: silvery means it is thirsty; green means it still has water.
  4. No water in the center of the leaves: the crown will rot.
  5. To get it to bloom again: cut the spike above the second or third node when the flowers drop and give it slightly cooler nights.

If you prefer cut flowers

Cut orchids are also sold (mostly cymbidium and dendrobium) for bouquets and centerpieces. If you want the same elegant effect in a bouquet, they go well with lilies, calla lilies or white roses.

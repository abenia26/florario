What Florario is

Florario (flor + calendario, “flower” + “calendar” in Spanish) is a free calendar with every date of the year on which it is customary to give flowers in Spain and Latin America: which flower is given, in which countries and why, with the seasonal flowers of each month. It brings together both the long-standing dates, such as Valentine’s Day, Sant Jordi or Mother’s Day, and the trends born on social media, such as the yellow flowers of September 21, the blue flowers of October 3 or the purple flowers of November 9. It is an independent project by {{author}}: it doesn’t sell flowers or charge anyone to be featured.

Florario in figures

  • {{n_events}} dates for giving flowers: {{n_clasico}} classics, {{n_trend}} social media trends and {{n_memoria}} remembrance dates.
  • {{n_songs}} dates born from a song, such as “Flores amarillas” from Floricienta or Cecilia’s “Un ramito de violetas”.
  • {{n_guides}} guides by date or occasion and {{n_flowers}} flower pages with their meaning and season.
  • {{n_countries}} countries with their own page and seasonal flowers for three regions: Spain, Mexico and Central America, and the Southern Cone.

All the figures come from the calendar itself and update automatically when a date is added. Every trend is checked against at least two media outlets from different countries; the method is explained in About Florario.

Stories ahead of each date

If you’re preparing a piece about a specific date, these guides cover the origin, the countries and which flowers are given, with sources at the bottom:

  • Yellow flowers (September 21) and their spring version, on March 21.
  • Blue flowers and Boyfriend Day (October 3).
  • Purple flowers (November 9).
  • All Saints’ Day and the Day of the Dead, the poinsettia and Our Lady of Guadalupe.
  • Mother’s Day in each country, which falls on four different dates.

The queen: the poinsettia

The poinsettia is the Christmas plant par excellence. It is native to Mexico, where the Mexica called it cuetlaxóchitl, and it reached the United States and Europe in the 19th century thanks to the diplomat Joel Poinsett, which is why it is called poinsettia in English. December 8 is National Poinsettia Day in Mexico:

What look like red petals are bracts, leaves that change color as the nights get longer. Besides the classic red there are white, pink, salmon and marbled poinsettias.

Other Christmas flowers and plants

  • Amaryllis: huge red or white trumpet-shaped flowers on a tall stem. It is given as a bulb and blooms in a few weeks, just in time for the holidays.
  • Hellebore or Christmas rose: it blooms in midwinter, with white or pinkish flowers. It symbolizes hope in the coldest days.
  • Cyclamen: a houseplant with pink, red or white flowers that lasts all winter in a cool spot.
  • Christmas cactus: it blooms in December with hanging pink or red flowers, and does so again every year.
  • Holly and mistletoe: more branches than flowers, for wreaths and centerpieces. Mistletoe is the one with the kissing tradition.
  • White orchid: elegant, lasts for weeks and goes with any decor. There is a guide to the orchid.

Bouquets to give at Christmas

If you prefer cut flowers, Christmas bouquets usually combine red or white roses, ranunculus, anemones or amaryllis with fir, eucalyptus, pine cones, cinnamon or hypericum berries. In December, in Spain, these are in season:

  • Anemones, ranunculus and hellebores.
  • Amaryllis and cyclamen.
  • Greenhouse roses and carnations, which in December come mostly from Colombia and Ecuador.

What the Christmas colors mean

Red is joy, love and celebration; white, peace and purity; green, hope and life that goes on in winter. There is more in the guides to red flowers and white flowers.

Tips for giving flowers at Christmas

  1. A plant beats a bouquet: a poinsettia or an amaryllis lasts the whole season; a bouquet may not make it to New Year’s Eve.
  2. Watch out for cold on the way home: the poinsettia drops its leaves if it gets cold, even briefly. Ask for it to be well wrapped.
  3. Away from heating: dry heat shortens the life of any flower.
  4. If you have pets, keep holly and mistletoe out of their reach: their berries are toxic.

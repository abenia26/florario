What each carnation color means

The carnation speaks of love and admiration, with nuances depending on the color:

  • Pink: a mother’s love and gratitude. It is the most popular color on Mother’s Day.
  • Red: deep admiration and passion. In Spain it is tied to flamenco and festivals; in Portugal, to freedom.
  • White: pure affection, luck and remembrance. It was the first Mother’s Day carnation, and in some countries it is worn in tribute to a mother who has died.
  • Fuchsia and two-tone: joy and energy; very fashionable in modern bouquets.
  • Yellow: in the Victorian language of flowers it meant disappointment. Hardly anyone reads it that way today, but if you want to avoid any double meaning, choose another color.

The carnation and Mother’s Day

The story of the carnation as the flower of mothers begins in the US. In 1908, Anna Jarvis organized a tribute to her mother and handed out white carnations, her favorite flower. A flower with history in Spain and Portugal

The carnation is native to the Mediterranean basin and has been grown since ancient times. In Spain it is the flower of the embroidered shawl, of the feria and of traditional song: without being the official national flower, it is the one most associated with the country.

In Portugal it carries even more weight. On April 25, 1974, the soldiers who overthrew the dictatorship took to the streets and people put red carnations in the barrels of their rifles. The Carnation Revolution is commemorated every year and the red carnation is still the symbol of that day.

Today Colombia is the world’s largest carnation exporter: its greenhouses on the Bogotá savanna supply florists across half the planet all year round.

How to make carnations last three weeks

  1. Cut the stem at an angle just above a node (the swellings on the stem): water rises less easily through the node.
  2. Remove the lower leaves, and the small side buds if you want a bigger main flower.
  3. Change the water every two or three days. Carnations are sensitive to ethylene, so keep them away from fruit.
  4. Avoid heat: in a cool spot they last twice as long.

Tip: a well-chosen bunch of carnations costs less than a bouquet of roses and lasts twice as long. For gifts that need to go the distance, like Mother’s Day or visiting someone in hospital, it is a safe bet.

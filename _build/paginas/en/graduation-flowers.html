Which flowers to give at a graduation

  • Sunflowers: they face the sun, so they symbolize looking ahead and a bright future. The most popular choice in Latin America.
  • Gerberas: cheerful, brightly colored and very resistant to the heat of the ceremony.
  • Yellow or orange roses: success, friendship and enthusiasm, without romantic undertones.
  • Tulips: a fresh, modern, colorful bouquet.
  • Orchid: for someone starting work or moving; a gift that lasts in the new apartment or office.
  • Red roses: only if the graduate is your partner.

The colors of achievement

Yellow is the star color: joy, success and friendship. There is more about it in yellow flowers. Orange conveys enthusiasm and energy; blue, confidence and calm about what comes next; and mixed bouquets are pure celebration. A touch people love: using the colors of the university or school.

When graduations take place

  • Spain and Mexico: from late May to July, peaking in June.
  • Colombia and Central America: it depends on each university’s calendar; many hold graduations in June and December.
  • Argentina, Chile, Uruguay and Peru: in November and December, at the end of the school year.
  • US: in May and June.

In summer, sunflowers are in full season in the northern hemisphere: prettier and cheaper.

Tips for a good graduation bouquet

  1. Compact and light: the graduate will hold it in photos, along with the gown, cap and diploma.
  2. Heat-resistant: sunflowers, gerberas, carnations and roses hold up better than delicate flowers.
  3. No staining pollen: if you include lilies, make sure the stamens are removed.
  4. With a note: a few words about the effort of these years are worth more than the size of the bouquet.
  5. Don’t forget the teachers: a flower for whoever helped most is a gesture people remember. See Teachers’ Day.

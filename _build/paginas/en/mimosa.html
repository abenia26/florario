What mimosa means

Mimosa is a flower of contrasts: its branches are tough and grow in difficult terrain, but its flowers are soft little pompoms that fall apart at the slightest touch. That’s why it is associated with strength and sensitivity at the same time. Its yellow also announces the end of winter: it is one of the first flowers of the year.

Mimosa and March 8 in Italy

In 1946, just after the war, the Union of Italian Women was looking for a symbol for the first International Women’s Day in freedom. They wanted a flower that bloomed in early March, was cheap and grew everywhere, so anyone could give it. Mimosa was the one chosen. Since then, every March 8 in Italy a sprig of mimosa is given to mothers, colleagues, friends and coworkers.

In Spain and Latin America, 8M is above all a day of protest. If you want to give flowers that day, there are ideas and context in the Women’s Day guide.

Mimosa season

In Spain it blooms between January and March; in Galicia, Asturias or on the Mediterranean coast whole hillsides turn yellow in February. In Chile it is called aromo and blooms in the middle of the southern winter, between July and August.

A beautiful but invasive flower

Mimosa is native to southeastern Australia and Tasmania. It arrived in Europe as an ornamental plant in the 19th century and adapted so well that today it is considered an invasive species in several parts of Spain and Portugal, especially in Galicia: it spreads fast, displaces native vegetation and resprouts vigorously after fires. That’s why planting it in the garden isn’t recommended in those regions, although its cut branches are still a lovely gift.

How to make it last

  1. Buy it with the pompoms still closed or half open: they will open at home.
  2. Trim the branches and put them in lukewarm water. The warmth helps the pompoms open.
  3. A humid environment: mimosa dries out very fast with the heating on. Misting a little water around it (not on the flowers) helps.
  4. About three to five days is normal in a vase. Afterwards, instead of throwing it away, dry it.

Dried mimosa

Hang the branches upside down in a dry, ventilated place out of direct sun. In one or two weeks they will be dry and keep their yellow color for months. They look great in ceramic vases or combined with dried eucalyptus, wheat or lavender.

Tip: its pollen can bother people with allergies. If the gift is for someone with spring allergies, choose yellow tulips, which are the 8M flower in Russia and Eastern Europe.

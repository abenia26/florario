What it means to give tulips

The tulip speaks of sincere love, less solemn than that of the red rose. The color adds nuance to the message:

  • Red: love and declaration, an alternative to the rose on Valentine’s Day.
  • Pink: affection and happiness, ideal for friends, mothers and birthdays.
  • Yellow: joy and good cheer. In the past it was associated with hopeless love; today it’s used in the yellow flowers trend.
  • White: forgiveness, calm and new beginnings.
  • Purple: admiration and elegance; it fits purple flowers day.

A bouquet of mixed tulips, like the one in the photo, is one of the easiest gifts to get right: it has no obligatory romantic reading and looks cheerful in any home.

When tulips are given

On 8M the tulip is the tradition in Russia and Eastern Europe, and on Galentine’s Day it is the flower seen most in group photos.

Tulip mania

The tulip is native to the mountains of Central Asia. The Ottomans made it a symbol of luxury and in the 16th century it reached Europe. In the 17th-century Netherlands, bulbs of the rarest varieties, with streaked petals, sold for outrageous prices. That frenzy, tulip mania, collapsed in 1637 and today it is cited as one of the first speculative bubbles in history. Ironically, those prized streaks were caused by a virus.

Today the Netherlands is still the world’s biggest producer and exports greenhouse tulips almost all year round.

How to care for a bunch of tulips

  1. A little cold water: about 10 cm in the vase is enough. Change it every two days.
  2. Trim 1 or 2 cm straight or at an angle when you get home and remove the lower leaves.
  3. A tall, narrow vase: tulips keep growing by up to 2 or 3 cm after being cut and seek the light; a vase that holds them up stops them from sprawling.
  4. Turn the vase every day so they don’t all lean towards the window.
  5. Buy them closed: with the bud still greenish at the tip they’ll last longer.

Trick: if they’ve already drooped, wrap them tightly and straight in newspaper and leave them in cold water for a few hours; they straighten up.

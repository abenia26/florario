The flower of each month

In the UK and the US each month has its flower, just as it has its stone. These are the most widely accepted:

Birth month flowers (Anglo-Saxon tradition)
MonthFlowerWhat it symbolizes
JanuaryCarnation and snowdropLove, admiration and hope
FebruaryViolet and primroseFaithfulness, modesty and youth
MarchDaffodilNew beginnings and rebirth
AprilDaisy and sweet peaInnocence, joy and a happy goodbye
MayLily of the valley and hawthornHappiness, luck and hope
JuneRose and honeysuckleLove and the bonds that unite
JulyLarkspur and water lilyJoy, lightness and purity
AugustGladiolus and poppyStrength, integrity and remembrance
SeptemberAster and morning gloryWisdom, patience and affection
OctoberMarigold and cosmosWarmth, creativity and harmony
NovemberChrysanthemumFriendship and long life (outside Spain)
DecemberNarcissus (paperwhite) and hollyHope, good wishes and protection

Where the tradition comes from

Birth month flowers are cousins of birthstones. The custom became popular in Victorian England, when the language of flowers was in fashion and every flower carried a message. The British and American lists don’t fully match, which is why several months have two flowers: you can choose the one you like best or the one that is easiest to find.

How to adapt it if you live in Spain or Latin America

The list was born in the climate of northern Europe, so some flowers don’t match what florists here have in that month:

  • November: in Spain, France and Italy the chrysanthemum is linked to the cemetery because of All Saints’ Day. Choose a dahlia, a rose or a brightly colored potted chrysanthemum.
  • October: “marigold” can mean calendula or the Mexican cempasúchil, and in Mexico the latter is the flower of the Day of the Dead. Calendula or cosmos are more neutral choices.
  • Southern hemisphere: in Argentina, Chile or Uruguay the seasons are reversed. If the month’s flower isn’t available, look for a seasonal “equivalent” on each month’s page.

Ideas for using their birth flower

  • For a birthday: a bouquet with their birth flower and a note explaining why you chose it.
  • Tattoos: a bouquet of the flowers of the months your children, partner or parents were born is one of the most requested designs.
  • Jewelry and prints: pendants engraved with the flower, illustrated prints or pressed flowers in a frame.
  • Baby showers and births: give the flower of the month the baby is born.
